# Cover research and design notes

Research date: 2 November 2026. Amazon live romance bestseller chart returned HTTP 503. Current Amazon ranks could not be verified. The following established bestselling/popular romance covers were reviewed through indexed cover images and retailer/editorial sources; this is not a claim that these are the current Amazon top ten.

## Cover references

1. The Love Hypothesis — Ali Hazelwood: bold legible lettering and a clear relationship premise.
2. The Hating Game — Sally Thorne: office cues and high-contrast playful visual tension.
3. The Flatshare — Beth O’Leary: spatial separation conveys forced proximity.
4. The Unhoneymooners — Christina Lauren: exuberant botanical color and strong title contrast.
5. People We Meet on Vacation — Emily Henry: warm color and economical setting cues.
6. The Spanish Love Deception — Elena Armas: large typography and small narrative accents.
7. It Happened One Summer — Tessa Bailey: immediately recognizable atmosphere and romantic pairing.
8. Act Your Age, Eve Brown — Talia Hibbert: inviting illustrated tone and lively color.
9. The Seven Year Slip — Ashley Poston: magical-romance cues and typographic movement.
10. The Dead Romantics — Ashley Poston: whimsical supernatural motifs with warmth.
11. Beach Read — Emily Henry: bright setting-led illustration and clear hierarchy.
12. Love and Other Words — Christina Lauren: intimate object imagery for emotional romance.

## Originality and source handling

The workbook provides ten English titles, author names, synopses and keywords. The final isolated NOVELSEA row is not a book record. Chinese series titles are not translated or invented on covers. No previous cover designs were present in the supplied folder, so comparison with earlier work was unavailable. Each new concept deliberately changes its medium, composition, motif and palette across the set. Reference covers inform legibility and genre signals only; no artwork or layout is copied.

English prompts are in the companion prompt document. Images use the built-in image generation tool, one book at a time.
